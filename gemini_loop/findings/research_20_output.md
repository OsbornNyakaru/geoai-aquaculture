## Key Findings

**1. Your binormal algebra is correct; I reproduce it independently.** With P=188, N=121, AUC=0.946460: Φ⁻¹(0.94646)=1.611, so the equal-variance binormal d′=√2·1.611=2.278. At your realized operating FPR=20/121=0.1653 (u=Φ⁻¹(0.165)=−0.974), the binormal TPR=Φ(2.278−0.974)=Φ(1.304)=0.904 → 170.0 TP. You realize 164 TP. **Conclusion (1) holds: your ROC is ~6 TP worse than a featureless curve of the same area at your own operating FPR** — your extra AUC area is banked in FPR regions the F1 metric never touches. The binormal F1 is maximized near u=−0.85 (pred≈197.5, F1≈0.9007), confirming conclusion (2): optimal cut placement on your EXISTING ranking is worth ≈+0.019 F1 → ≈+0.011 composite. And the leader's (FPR≈0.124, TPR≈0.920) yields F1≈0.920, which exceeds the binormal maximum (~0.900) at AUC 0.9449 — confirming conclusion (3): the leader's ROC has a genuine elbow, unreachable on any smooth curve of that area.

**2. The leader's operating point IS achievable at AUC 0.9449 — under a non-binormal ROC.** The published operating-point/AUC bound (geometry-of-ROC result, arXiv:2504.02169) gives se·sp ≤ AUROC ≤ se+sp−se·sp. For the leader (se=0.920, sp=0.876): 0.806 ≤ AUROC ≤ 0.990. Their 0.9449 sits comfortably inside, so their point is consistent with their area — it just requires a ranking with a well-separated confident core plus a small confusable set (equivalently an unequal-variance / "proper" binormal ROC per Metz & Pan 1999, J. Math. Psychol. 43(1):1–33; or a bi-beta ROC). This is a MODELING difference, not a thresholding one. Some of the leader's advantage is therefore genuinely NOT reachable at any threshold on a smooth curve of your area — it lives in ROC shape.

**3. There is NO published closed-form bound for max-F1 as a function of (AUC, prevalence, n).** The relevant published bound (Zhao, Edakunni, Pocock & Brown, "Beyond Fano's Inequality: Bounds on the Optimal F-Score, BER, and Cost-Sensitive Risk," JMLR 14:1033–1090, 2013) bounds the optimal F-score via *conditional entropy / Bayes error rate*, not via AUC. Lipton et al. (arXiv:1402.1892, ECML-PKDD 2014, LNCS 8725:225–239) relate the optimal *threshold* to the optimal F-value (F*/2), not max-F1 to AUC. The binormal/proper-ROC computation above is therefore the best available estimate; treat its numbers as model-based, not as a theorem.

**4. Under mixed covariate+conditional shift, no valid train-only prevalence estimator exists.** MLLS (Alexandari, Kundaje & Shrikumar, ICML 2020, PMLR 119:222–232), BBSE (Lipton, Wang & Smola, arXiv:1802.03916, ICML 2018), Saerens–Latinne–Decaestecker EM ("Adjusting the Outputs of a Classifier to New a Priori Probabilities," Neural Computation 14(1):21–41, Jan 2002, DOI 10.1162/089976602753284446), and ACC/PACC/Median-Sweep (Forman, "Quantifying counts and costs via classification," Data Min. Knowl. Disc. 17(2):164–206, 2008, DOI 10.1007/s10618-008-0097-y) ALL assume p(x|y) is invariant (pure label / prior-probability shift). Your KS goodness-of-fit gate (D=0.186, p≈0) rejects that assumption, so all of them are invalid here and share the SAME low-bias family — this is not "two estimators agreeing," it is two members of one invalid family sharing one bias. Tasche ("Class Prior Estimation under Covariate Shift: No Problem?", arXiv:2206.02449, and "Invariance assumptions for class distribution estimation," arXiv:2311.17225) proves that even under *pure* covariate shift, classify-and-count-style estimators are infeasible unless the covariate transform is statistically sufficient for the full covariate set. **The operating-point lane is therefore UNDECIDABLE, not closed — the "no gap" conclusion is unsupported in BOTH directions.**

**5. The linear-pool defect is quantitatively negligible at ρ≈0.98.** Knüppel & Krüger, "Forecast uncertainty, disagreement, and the linear pool," Journal of Applied Econometrics 37(1):23–41, Jan 2022 (DOI 10.1002/jae.2834), give a closed-form over-dispersion equal to 2·E[disagreement] ∝ (1−ρ²)(1+ρ); the paper states the linear pool "is always more accurate for several proper scoring rules than the worst-performing component forecast." At ρ=0.98 the factor (1−ρ²)=0.0396 — ~4% of the decorrelated maximum. This matches your empirical finding that pooled pos-rates sit AT or ABOVE the member mean, the opposite of a biting compression defect. The root cause in the binary calibration proof (Ranjan & Gneiting 2010, JRSS-B 72(1):71–91, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9868.2009.00726.x) is the pairwise diversity term E(pᵢ−pⱼ)², which →0 as members coincide.

**6. Your ARM T anomaly is threshold brittleness, NOT the Ranjan–Gneiting defect and NOT "underdispersion makes pooling worse."** The calibration literature (Gneiting & Ranjan, "Combining Predictive Distributions," Electron. J. Statist. 7:1747–1782, 2013, arXiv:1106.1638) proves linear pooling ADDS dispersion, which HELPS underdispersed members — so pooled-F1-below-both-members is not a calibration failure. It is a hard-0.5-cut artifact: the cross-view logit-variance penalty compresses logits toward a constant, piling probability mass near 0.5; averaging keeps mass there; near-boundary mass flips easily under the hard cut, so pooled F1 can fall below both members while pooled AUC (threshold-free) lands normally between them. Underdispersion IS the right predictor — of threshold brittleness, diagnosed by score density / PIT-histogram mass near 0.5 (Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, "Probabilistic forecasts, calibration and sharpness," JRSS-B 69(2):243–268, 2007).

**7. F1↑/AUC↓ from consistency regularization is a real, documented, intrinsic trade.** Entropy-minimization/consistency methods (Grandvalet & Bengio, NIPS 2004) sharpen predictions toward the simplex corners; this improves argmax/threshold metrics while leaving AUC flat or slightly degraded because indiscriminate confidence corrupts the global ordering that AUC measures. This is a property of the entropy objective, not of λ_u tuning alone.

**8. The central compliance question does not have a clean answer.** Koyejo, Natarajan, Ravikumar & Dhillon ("Consistent Binary Classification with Generalized Performance Metrics," NIPS 2014, pp. 2744–2752) prove the F1-optimal classifier IS EXACTLY a thresholded posterior sign(η(x)−δ*) with δ* depending on the optimal F1 value. So at the population optimum, sigmoidF1 training and "BCE then threshold at δ*" reach the SAME decision region — making F1-surrogate training *extensionally* a way of selecting a non-0.5 posterior threshold from training data. It is defensible as legal (it changes the estimand via a published loss and references no realized outcome), but it is NOT cleanly distinct from threshold selection, so it carries genuine code-review risk.

## Details

### Q1(a)–(c): Is the F1 gap cut-placement or local ranking?

The decisive number: the equal-variance binormal MAX F1 at your AUC 0.9465 is ≈0.9007. Your champion pool realizes F1 0.8895. So:

- **Leader's 0.920 is ABOVE the binormal ceiling.** Reaching it is impossible by cut placement on your current ranking; it requires a sharper local ROC (the elbow). +0.031 F1 at your AUC by cut placement alone is NOT attainable.

**Answer to Q1(a):** the achievable F1@0.5 range at AUC≈0.945, prevalence≈0.61, n=1,030 is roughly [0.86, 0.90] on a smooth (binormal) ranking; your 0.882/0.889 sits at the top of the smooth-curve band, the leader's 0.920 sits OUTSIDE it (kinked ROC). The gap is therefore ~40% cut-placement (recoverable legally) and ~60% local ranking (a discrimination problem no compliant one-slot move closes).

**Answer to Q1(b) — a train-only diagnostic separating the two, with no LB-inverted quantity.** Compute on your regime-matched OOF (already masked to the test-window distribution, per src/seq_model.py:980–981):
1. The empirical F1-optimal threshold t* on OOF vs 0.5. If t*≪0.5 with a large F1 gap between F1(t*) and F1(0.5), your cut is mis-placed (fixable). If t*≈0.5 already, cut placement is not your problem.
2. The local ROC slope (empirical likelihood ratio) in the FPR band [0.12,0.20] on OOF vs a binormal fit of the same OOF AUC. If the empirical curve tracks the binormal, you are ranking-limited; if it has a kink you are not exploiting, that is reachable.
3. Score-density comparison near 0.5 on test vs OOF. **Because your OOF level (~0.975) is anti-correlated with LB level (~0.90), TRUST SHAPE STATISTICS (t*, local slope, density kurtosis near the cut) and DISTRUST level statistics (OOF F1/AUC magnitude). Shape transfers; level does not.** This is load-bearing for any OOF-based kill condition and is defensible in code review precisely because it reads shape, not level.

**Answer to Q1(c) — partial AUC.** Your operating point (FPR 0.12–0.17) is mid-upper ROC, NOT the small-FPR screening regime that essentially all pAUC literature targets. The one paper covering a mid-range band is Yao, Lin & Yang, "Large-scale Optimization of Partial AUC in a Range of False Positive Rates" (NeurIPS 2022, 35:31239–31253; arXiv:2203.01505v2, 27 Oct 2022), which trains over FPR∈[0.05,0.5] by casting range-restricted pAUC as "a non-smooth difference-of-convex (DC) program for any smooth predictive functions" — the only published support for a band overlapping yours. Two-way pAUC (Yang, Xu, Bao, He, Cao & Huang, TPAMI 2022 — **NOTE: the brief's arXiv:2206.11433 is a citation error; the correct ID is arXiv:2206.11655**) constrains both TPR≥α and FPR≤β and is the right conceptual tool, but its surrogates need CVaR/DRO machinery (LibAUC) and large batches, and the literature reports overfitting when the band is narrow and positives are scarce. **Verdict: pAUC/TPAUC is NOT implementable to convergence in one 25-minute slot on a 71k-param model with 1,817 rows, and its training instability (not its compliance) is disqualifying for Round 20.** For completeness on compliance: the band COULD be fixed from the OOF operating point without touching test/LB, which would be legal — but that does not rescue the trainability problem. Fix the Round-19 bibliography omission by recording arXiv:2203.01505 and 2206.11655 as "read, not adopted, on trainability grounds."

### Q2: The linear pool

### The compliance adjudication (the round's central question)

Your restated prong (c) — "corrects a demonstrable mismatch between what the model optimizes and what the metric rewards, without reference to realized outcomes" — DOES admit sigmoidF1 in letter: BCE's Bayes-optimal output is p(y|x), whose F1-optimal cut is F*/2≈0.45. This is verbatim from the Lipton, Elkan & Naryanaswamy abstract (arXiv:1402.1892): "if the classifier outputs are well-calibrated conditional probabilities, then the optimal threshold is half the optimal F1 value" (note: the optimal F1 *value*, not the score you realize). So every BCE artifact you have submitted is evaluated at a point its loss never optimized. Training with sigmoidF1 (Bénédict, Koops, Odijk & de Rijke, arXiv:2108.10566, published in Trans. Mach. Learn. Res.) moves the 0.5 level-set toward the F1-optimal contour.

BUT the Koyejo/Narasimhan theory (NIPS 2014) proves the F1-optimal classifier is EXACTLY a thresholded posterior sign(η(x)−δ*). At the population optimum, sigmoidF1's decision region equals {x: p(y|x)≥δ*}, δ*≈0.45 — identical to "BCE then threshold at 0.45." So the two are extensionally the same rule, and a reviewer can argue sigmoidF1 is a disguised, train-data-selected threshold move.

**My honest verdict: sigmoidF1 is LEGAL-BUT-CONTESTABLE.** It IS distinct from the Round-19 §2.3 score-shift (which relabeled a FIXED score distribution so 0.5 "behaves like 0.46" — clearly a post-hoc estimate move that prong (c) correctly kills) because it changes what the network estimates DURING TRAINING rather than relabeling a frozen estimate. But it is NOT distinct from threshold selection *at the optimum*, because the consistency theory says the F-measure-optimal classifier just IS a thresholded posterior. Your prong (c) restatement is a genuine improvement — it correctly separates "change the estimand" from "relabel an estimate" — but it cannot fully separate F1-surrogate training from threshold choice, because the theory says the two coincide at the optimum. Present it to code review with BOTH the F*/2 mechanism AND the Koyejo counterargument stated explicitly; do not present it as unambiguously compliant.

**Evidence that F1-surrogate helps at a FIXED threshold (the claim that must hold):** the sigmoidF1 abstract reports "sigmoidF1 outperforms other loss functions on one text and two image datasets and several metrics" — but those gains are measured at tuned inference points, not at a pre-specified 0.5. There is THIN direct evidence that a sigmoidF1 model beats BCE *specifically at a fixed 0.5 cut without any threshold search*. This is the weakest link in the proposal and you must state it plainly. On the AUC cost: F-surrogates that concentrate on the decision boundary can shed a small amount of ranking quality; you have AUC headroom (0.9465 > leader's 0.9449), so a loss of ≤0.003 AUC is affordable, but the magnitude is not well characterized in the literature for this setting.
