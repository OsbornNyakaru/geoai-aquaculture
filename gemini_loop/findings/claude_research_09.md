# Round #09 Strategy: Reaching Top-2 in the FAO/ITU/Zindi GeoAI Aquaculture Pond Challenge

## TL;DR
- The highest-EV move is NOT another architecture tweak — it is a **variance-collapse submission** (many seeds × 5 folds × decorrelated architectures, combined by rank-averaging) whose value is invisible on the public LB but materially raises your PRIVATE rank via order statistics, because most of the field is submitting single-seed upward fluctuations (seed sd ≈0.019).
- Only three ideas plausibly clear the ~0.02 level bar and all are model-class or representation-class moves: **in-domain transductive SSL** (masked-value pretraining on train+test unlabeled — the one legal way to let the encoder see the shifted test distribution), **dispersion-aware pooling** (physics says a pond is a permanently low-backscatter, low-temporal-variance cell — a signal your mean-pool literally discards), and **a decorrelated random-kernel member (MiniRocket/Hydra)** that the ensemble-diversity theory predicts will buy level, not just variance.
- I challenge findings (A), (B) and (E): the ≥0.02 bar is the correct filter for *level* ideas but the *wrong* filter for *variance* ideas (which win on private without raising level); "irreducible covariate shift" under a rank-only metric does not require distribution matching, only rank transfer, so high adversarial AUC does not prove the ranking is untransferable; and seed-averaging landed at the member mean *precisely because* members were 95.1%-correlated — the DiWA/model-soup literature predicts decorrelated members WILL gain OOD level.

## Key Findings

**The competition structure changes the objective.** The Zindi challenge page confirms a two-phase evaluation: Phase One is the LB score (65% of final, with 1st→10 pts … 5th→6 pts) and Phase Two is a rubric on innovation + reproducibility for the **top 5 only** (35%). Consequences you may be under-weighting: (1) you must finish top-5 on the *private* LB for anything else to matter; (2) the gap between 1st (10 pts) and 5th (6 pts) is only 4 of 65 LB points, while a strong novel + reproducible writeup is worth up to 9–10 of 35 rubric points — so over-optimizing the last 0.005 of LB is a mistake relative to securing top-5 + a defensible novelty story; (3) the rules mandate seeded reproducibility ("Always set the seed. Rerunning your model should always place you at the same position"), which independently rewards low-variance pipelines. Rules also confirm pretrained models are legal ("You may use pretrained models as long as they are openly available to everyone"), external data banned, AutoML banned, 5 subs/day, 100 total, 2 finals selected, public ≈30% / private ≈70%.

**The rank-only proof (validates D, with one refinement).** After the legal monotone prevalence pin fixes the predicted-positive count P̂ = #{rows scored ≥ 0.5}, and with true test positive count P, F1 = 2·TP/(P̂ + P). With the ranking fixed, TP = P̂ · precision@P̂, so F1 = 2·P̂·prec@P̂ / (P̂+P) is a functional of *which rows fall in the top-P̂* — i.e., of the ranking alone. ROC-AUC is rank-only by definition. Therefore any temperature, constant logit offset, focal loss, or label-smoothing effect is invisible to the LB except through side effects on the order. **Refinement:** the pin itself IS visible, because choosing the monotone map to hit a target P̂ changes which rows cross 0.5. So "calibration is invisible" is exactly right; "the operating point is invisible" is not.

**The order-statistics case for variance reduction (the central strategic insight).** Model each near-top competitor's submitted model as having latent quality Q (a draw over configs/seeds) with public score = Q + u and private score = Q + v, where u, v are *independent* finite-sample noises on the 309-row public and 721-row private sets. A competitor who makes n public submissions and keeps the best is selecting on u: their public score is inflated by E[max of n draws] (for σ≈0.019 and ~3–5 effective independent draws, roughly +0.02 to +0.03). That inflation does not carry to private because v is fresh. Hence the public "top ≈0.9452" is very plausibly an upward fluctuation, and the honest private level of the public leader may sit ~0.915–0.925. If YOU collapse your own quality variance (heavy seed×fold×architecture averaging), your submitted model sits reliably at your true mean μ while single-seed rivals scatter with sd σ_v on private. The probability you beat rival j on private, with your variance ≈0, is Φ((μ − μ_j)/σ_v); your expected private rank ≈ 1 + Σ_j Φ((μ_j − μ)/σ_v). Because the top of the field is tightly packed (rank-50 ≈0.876, top-5 ≈0.928–0.945, your reliable ≈0.8865), even small reductions in your σ move many ranks, and rivals who led on public via positive u regress. This is why the correct final-selection policy is to pick your **lowest-variance** submission, not your highest public one.

**Ensembling: why seeds failed and decorrelated architectures should not (challenges E).** For M equicorrelated members with mean pairwise correlation ρ, the ensemble's variance-reduction factor is (1 + ρ(M−1))/M. At ρ=0.951, M=5 this is (1+3.804)/5 = 0.96 — a 4% reduction, i.e., you land essentially at the member mean, exactly what you observed. At ρ=0.7, M=5 it is 0.76 (24% reduction); at ρ=0.5 it is 0.60 (40%). Rame et al. (DiWA, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.09739) prove via a bias–variance–covariance–locality decomposition that weight-averaging gains *specifically under diversity/covariate shift* (the variance term dominates); their exact statement is "the lower the covariance, the greater the gain of WA over its members… It motivates tackling covariance by encouraging members to make different predictions, thus to be functionally diverse," and the decomposition shows "WA succeeds when the variance term dominates, which… occurs when the marginal distribution changes at test time." Wortsman et al. (Model Soups, ICML 2022, arXiv:2203.05482) report the OOD gain of soups *exceeds* the in-distribution gain: their Table 1 ViT-G/14 greedy soup scores 85.02 vs 84.68 for the best single model on the 5 distribution-shift sets (+0.34) while gaining only 90.94 vs 90.78 on ImageNet itself (+0.16) — a larger OOD than ID gain, the signature of a shift-specific benefit. Ovadia et al. (NeurIPS 2019) independently find "Deep ensembles seem to perform the best across most metrics and be more robust to dataset shift," and that "relatively small ensemble size (e.g. M = 5) may be sufficient." **The go/no-go this yields:** only add an ensemble member whose mean pairwise rank-correlation with the existing pool is below ~0.9 (target 0.6–0.75) and whose solo ATC-F1 is within ~0.02 of champion.

**Rank-averaging is the provably correct combiner under a rank-only metric.** Final scores by rank-averaging, s(x) = (1/M) Σ_m rank_m(x), are invariant to any per-member monotone reparameterization g_m of member scores. Probability-averaging (1/M) Σ p_m(x) is NOT: a member with a wide, spread score distribution dominates a member whose probabilities are compressed into [0.4, 0.6], so probability-averaging silently up-weights the more-spread (not the more-accurate) member. Logit-averaging is worse — sensitive to each member's logit scale/temperature. Since your members are individually miscalibrated in *different* ways under the shift, only rank-averaging neutralizes the differential miscalibration. This is a free, provable improvement over the probability-averaging most competitors default to.

**The shift is real and large but the fatalism is partly wrong (challenges B).** The evidence for the shift is strong and I accept it: adversarial train-vs-test AUC ≈0.99 on hand features and 0.965–0.976 on frozen Presto embeddings, plus the stable ~0.085 OOF→LB gap. But adversarial AUC measures whether train and test are *distinguishable*, not whether the label-relevant *ordering* transfers. A feature can be perfectly train/test-separable yet still rank ponds correctly within each test scene (e.g., the absolute backscatter floor shifts between periods, but ponds remain the lowest-backscatter, lowest-variance cells within any period). Under a rank-only metric you do not need to match distributions — you need the within-test pond ordering to hold. The productive corollary: seek representations invariant to the nuisance (acquisition period / masking recipe) while *preserving within-series relative structure* (permanence, dispersion), which is exactly where dispersion-aware pooling and transductive consistency operate. This also explains the tension with finding (C): within-series *rank* replacement collapsed OOF 0.975→0.86 because it removed absolute level, which is real signal — but *dispersion* is a different within-series statistic that (unlike level) is plausibly period-invariant and is destroyed by mean-pooling.

**The physics (Q6).** The aquaculture-pond remote-sensing literature is consistent. Ottinger, Clauss & Kuenzer (Remote Sensing 2017, 9(5):440) map ponds across the China/Vietnam deltas at "an overall accuracy of 0.83 for the four study sites," and state that "the different backscatter responses of the pond components (dikes and enclosed water surface) and aquaculture's distinct rectangular structure allow for separation of aquaculture areas from other natural water bodies." Ponds are shallow, dike-enclosed, near-permanent water surfaces that appear as **persistently low SAR backscatter** (specular reflection from calm water) and are separated from natural/temporary water and rice paddies chiefly by *temporal permanence* (Prasad et al., Remote Sensing 2019 11(3):357; Ottinger et al., Remote Sensing 2022 14(1):153). Rice paddies, by contrast, show a pronounced seasonal backscatter swing — low at flooding/sowing, rising through the vegetative-to-heading stages, then changing again around drainage/harvest (Rice-Field Mapping with Sentinel-1A, Remote Sensing 2021 13(1):103; Saku case study, Remote Sensing 2022 14(14):3254). The discriminator is therefore this *temporal variance*: rice swings, ponds stay permanently low. So the pond signature is largely **order-free**: low LEVEL + low DISPERSION (permanence) + simultaneous SAR-low/optical-water co-behaviour. It is not strongly order-sensitive (the fill→grow→drain *sequence* matters for crop-type discrimination, less for pond-vs-not). Two structural consequences: (a) within-window dispersion (temporal std/percentiles) is a genuine discriminator that **mean-pooling destroys**; (b) within-month SAR/optical co-behaviour is already representable (the per-month linear layer mixes all 24 channels), so the gap is dispersion, not co-behaviour.

**Analogous competitions (Q5, primary sources).** In the ITU/FAO GeoAI Cropland Mapping challenge (the closest analog — designed region shift, S1+S2 monthly series; github.com/ITU-GeoAI-Challenge), *every* published top solution used GBDT (CatBoost/LightGBM/XGBoost) on hand-crafted per-month spectral-index features, and the shift-handling mechanism was **per-region separate models then concatenated predictions** (documented in the 3rd- and 6th-place repos), not pseudo-labeling. The EY 2023 Open Science Data Challenge (rice/non-rice, Vietnam, Sentinel-1) top artifacts used SAR temporal indices (VH/VV, RVI) plus multimodal fusion and deep ensembles. The "augment-training-to-match-test-missingness" mechanism is validated in the peer-reviewed literature rather than a named competition: Mask-PSTIN (Sun et al., Computers & Electronics in Agriculture 2023) uses *temporal random masking* of training series and reports outperforming Transformer and CNN-LSTM, especially with few timesteps; Presto and SITS-Former pretrain via masking and are explicitly robust to missing months/sensors. ROCKET did not appear in any winning writeup I found — a caution on its solo ceiling, though not on its ensemble-diversity value.

## Details: the candidate ledger, ranked

### FUND NOW

**1. Variance-collapse rank-averaged ensemble (private-LB optimizer).**
- *Mechanism.* Submit the mean *rank* over K seeds × 5 folds × the tied top-cluster architectures (relative-time, NoPE, λ=3, cross-view). Does not raise public level; raises reliable private rank per the order-statistics argument above.
- *Legal / not-a-repeat.* Supplied data only, seeded, reproducible. It is NOT "seed-averaging as a level lever" (already refuted) — it is deployed as *private-slice insurance*, its true and correct purpose, plus it satisfies the mandatory reproducibility rule.
- *Math.* Final score s(x) = (1/M) Σ_m rank_m(x); expected private rank ≈ 1 + Σ_j Φ((μ_j − μ)/σ_v). Include member m only if mean pairwise rank-corr ρ_m < 0.9. Combine by rank, not probability (proof above).
- *Effect / screen.* Level effect ≈0 (by design); private-rank effect is the payoff and is screened offline for zero submissions via ATC-F1 (ρ +0.964) and DIS (ρ +1.000). No submission needed to build; spend 1 submission to confirm the assembled ensemble's public score is within noise of champion.
- *Isolated experiment.* Champion vs rank-averaged multi-architecture pool; screenable for zero, 1 confirmation submission.
- *Sources.* DiWA (Rame et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.09739); Model Soups (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022, arXiv:2203.05482); Ovadia et al. (NeurIPS 2019); Kuncheva & Whitaker (Machine Learning 2003, 51(2):181–207) for the diversity measures; Lipton/Elkan/Naryanaswamy (arXiv:1402.1892) for the F1 geometry.

**2. In-domain transductive SSL pretraining (the one model-class-scale attack on the shift).**
- *Mechanism.* Masked-value modeling (MAE-style) on all 2,847 rows (1,817 train + 1,030 unlabeled test): randomly mask observed (month, band) entries beyond the natural missingness, encode with the SAME 24-channel transformer, decode masked standardized values. Then fine-tune the champion BCE + λ·Var head on the 1,817 labeled rows. Because pretraining sees the test rows unlabeled, it places them on the same manifold — directly attacking the shift, which Presto (frozen, never saw these rows) could not.
- *Legal / not-a-repeat.* Transductive use of the provided unlabeled test features is allowed (no external data, no labels). It is NOT "Presto frozen features" (dead): Presto encoded the shift because it was pretrained off-domain; this is pretrained IN-domain on the exact shifted distribution.
- *Math.* L_SSL = (1/|M|) Σ_{(t,b)∈M} (x̂_{t,b} − x_{t,b})² over artificially masked observed entries; then L_fine = BCE + λ·Var_k(logit), λ=1.0, warm-starting from the SSL encoder.
- *Effect / screen.* Plausibly ≥0.02 (model-class scale). **Free go/no-go:** compute adversarial train-vs-test AUC on the SSL fine-tuned embeddings; promote only if it drops materially below the 0.965 Presto floor (target <0.95). Then ATC-F1.
- *Isolated experiment.* Champion (from-scratch encoder) vs SSL-pretrained encoder, same head. Free adversarial gate first; 1 submission only if the gate passes.
- *Caveat.* 2,847 rows is tiny for SSL; mitigate with many masked views per row per epoch and heavy weight decay + early stopping. Honest risk: the encoder may memorize rather than generalize, in which case the gate fails and you stop for zero cost.
- *Sources.* Presto (Tseng et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2304.14065); TimeMAE (arXiv:2303.00320), Ti-MAE (arXiv:2301.08871); Test-Time Training with MAE (Gandelsman et al.); Mask-PSTIN (Sun et al., 2023); Garg et al. ATC (ICLR 2022, arXiv:2201.04234) for the gate.

### SCREEN FIRST

**3. Dispersion-aware pooling (physics-motivated, capacity-neutral).**
- *Mechanism.* Replace masked mean-pool with concatenated moments over observed months: mean ⊕ std ⊕ min ⊕ max (⊕ optional 10th/90th percentiles), computed per channel over the *observed* window only. Captures pond permanence (low dispersion) which mean-pooling discards.
- *Legal / not-a-repeat.* Amplitude-PRESERVING and channel-REPLACING at the pooling stage (not adding input channels, not instance-norm, not detrend, not differencing — all refuted). It changes the pooled representation, not the raw values.
- *Math.* Given per-month encoder outputs h_t for observed t, pool = [mean_t h_t ‖ std_t h_t ‖ min_t h_t ‖ max_t h_t] → MLP head. std computed only over non-missing months (mask-aware).
- *Effect / screen.* Plausibly ≥0.02 because dispersion is a *new* invariant that (unlike absolute level) is plausibly period-robust, so it should transfer under the shift. Screen with ATC-F1 (~0.010 resolution) and OOF; the transfer claim is testable via adversarial AUC on the dispersion features.
- *Isolated experiment.* Champion mean-pool vs moment-pool. One submission if ATC-F1 ≥ champion − 0.005.
- *Sources.* Ottinger, Clauss & Kuenzer (Remote Sensing 2017 9(5):440); Prasad et al. (2019 11(3):357); Ottinger et al. (2022 14(1):153); Sentinel-1 rice drainage phenology (Remote Sensing 2021 13(1):103; 2022 14(14):3254).

**4. Decorrelated random-kernel member: MiniRocket / Hydra.**
- *Mechanism.* Random convolutional kernels + ridge/logistic head. Shift-agnostic by construction (kernels are not learned) and low fitted capacity. Its value is as a *maximally decorrelated ensemble member*, per the DiWA/soup theory — even if its solo level is below champion.
- *Legal / not-a-repeat.* Openly available (sktime / `angus924` implementations), supplied data only, seeded. A genuine model-class departure from mean-pooled attention; not previously tried.
- *Input contract (the crux for masked series).* Do NOT feed −9999 (kernels would fire on the sentinel). Left-align the observed window to t=0 (relative-time reframing, already known helpful), edge-pad to length 12 with the last observed value, and append the 12 binary missing-indicator series as extra input channels so the ridge head can down-weight padded regions. Apply the SAME test masking distribution (K views) to training rows before the transform so kernels see train and test at matched observed density → MiniRocket PPV features → ridge/logistic.
- *Effect / screen.* Solo effect could be ±≥0.02. **Free screens:** adversarial AUC on ROCKET features, solo ATC-F1, then the cross-model rank-correlation with the transformer (include if ρ<0.75).
- *Isolated experiment.* MiniRocket solo (ATC-F1) → then rank-averaged with champion. Screenable for zero; 1 submission to confirm the blended public score.
- *Sources.* Dempster et al. ROCKET (DMKD 2020), MiniRocket (KDD 2021, arXiv:2102.00457 for MultiRocket), Hydra (DMKD 2023, arXiv:2203.13652); "Bake off redux" (Middlehurst et al., arXiv:2304.13029) for benchmark context.

**5. Transductive consistency + capped class-balanced pseudo-labeling.**
- *Mechanism A (near-free).* Extend the cross-view invariance penalty to UNLABELED test rows: L += λ_test · Var_k(logit) over masked views of test rows. Makes the encoder invariant to masking on the test distribution — transductively attacks the masking component of the shift.
- *Mechanism B (higher-risk).* One round of CBST-style pseudo-labeling: take the top-p test rows as pseudo-positives and bottom-p as pseudo-negatives with pseudo-prevalence pinned to ≈0.649 to prevent majority collapse; retrain including them; **cap at 1 iteration** (CBST empirically degrades by iteration 3). Fold-safety: generate pseudo-labels from a frozen first-round ensemble and hold them fixed across folds, so no fold sees test rows labeled by a model trained on that fold.
- *Legal / not-a-repeat.* Uses unlabeled test structurally, not via importance weighting (which is dead: ESS collapses at adv-AUC 0.99). Pseudo-labeling is reopened per the round brief (offline screen + transductive rationale + rank-only metric).
- *Math.* CBST class-balanced objective min_θ Σ_i ℓ(θ; x_i, ŷ_i) with per-class confidence thresholds set so selected pseudo-positive fraction ≈ pinned prevalence; soft-label / output-smoothing regularization (CRST) to bound overconfidence.
- *Effect / screen.* Uncertain; Mechanism A is cheap and may stack; Mechanism B risks confirmation bias (the confident test rows may be the shifted ones — CRST documents "overconfident label belief on wrong classes"). Under the rank-only metric only the *order* on confident rows must be right, a weaker and mildly favorable requirement. Screen with DIS (two-seed disagreement) and ATC-F1; abandon if worst-fold OOF drops.
- *Sources.* Zou et al. CBST (ECCV 2018) and CRST (ICCV 2019); Arazo et al. (confirmation bias in pseudo-labeling); EY 2023 organizer pseudo-labeling precedent; Garg et al. ATC (ICLR 2022).

**6. Group-DRO over the masking recipe (robustness knob).**
- *Mechanism.* Treat the masking recipe (window length 4/5/6 × start offset × worst-cloud-month present) as the group variable and minimize worst-group loss, hardening against the worst masking stratum without importance weighting.
- *Math.* min_θ max_{q∈Δ} Σ_g q_g (1/n_g) Σ_{i∈g} ℓ(θ; x_i, y_i), with online q_g ← q_g·exp(η·L_g) normalized (Sagawa et al., ICLR 2020), plus strong ℓ2/early stopping (required for Group-DRO to generalize).
- *Effect / screen.* Likely <0.02 (a robustness knob, not a level lever), because test masking is already drawn from the same distribution your K views sample. Screen offline; PARK unless worst-stratum OOF shows a large gap. Feeds the variance-reduction goal more than the level goal.
- *Sources.* Sagawa et al. (ICLR 2020, openreview ryxGuJrFvS); CVaR-DRO / χ²-DRO (Levy et al. 2020).

**7. Prevalence-pin re-derivation (cross-cutting must-do).**
- *Mechanism.* Re-derive P̂ per final model rather than reusing the single-seed sweep. The optimal-F1 condition: dF1/dP̂ = 0 ⟹ marginal precision at the cut p_marg(P̂) = F1/2 (Lipton/Elkan/Naryanaswamy 2014, "if the classifier outputs are well-calibrated conditional probabilities, then the optimal threshold is half the optimal F1 score"). Under prevalence uncertainty, maximize E_P[2·TP(P̂)/(P̂+P)] and, because the plateau is flat (LB flat over realized rate 0.59–0.67), pick the plateau center for robustness rather than the single-seed argmax.
- *Effect / screen.* The prior flat sweep is suspect (single-seed); re-derive on the seed-averaged OOF. Free (offline). Do it for every final submission.
- *Sources.* Lipton et al. (arXiv:1402.1892); the challenge's hard-0.5 rule.

### PARK

- **Other geospatial foundation models (Q2).** Prithvi (v1/v2), SatMAE/SatMAE++, Clay, CROMA, DOFA, AnySat, TerraMind, Major TOM-derived models are **spatial-patch** models — inapplicable, there is no spatial neighbourhood. Galileo has a pixel/time-series mode and is the only one worth a *free* adversarial-AUC screen, but it shares Presto's S1/S2 amplitude prior and will likely encode the shift too. Verdict: PARK behind in-domain SSL; if you screen one, screen Galileo's frozen features for zero cost.
- **Generic time-series foundation models (Q2).** Chronos/Chronos-Bolt, MOMENT, TimesFM are **univariate** — they cannot ingest the 12-band series jointly (per-band-then-concatenate loses the SAR/optical co-behaviour). MOIRAI is the only natively multivariate one (any-variate attention) but is a forecasting model with long-context assumptions; TTM (TinyTimeMixer) is multivariate via channel-mixing but short-context. A non-geospatial pretraining corpus could be *more* shift-invariant (no S1/S2 amplitude prior) but *less* task-relevant; net unclear. All gated by the free adversarial-AUC test; none obviously beats in-domain SSL. Fine-tuning any on 1,817 rows risks overfit, so frozen-feature mode only — and frozen features are what failed for Presto.
- **Path signatures / GP / state-space (S4, Mamba) (Q3).** Signatures are a genuine inductive-bias departure (order-sensitive, native irregular sampling, low capacity) but the pond signature is largely order-FREE, so signatures spend capacity on the wrong invariant; also imputation choice is fragile for shallow signature models (arXiv:2005.12359). Keep as a possible decorrelated member only if MiniRocket underdelivers. S4/Mamba are high-capacity for 1,817 rows.
- **Mixup / CutMix-temporal / band-dropout / FGSM augmentation.** Each <0.02; band-dropout is cheap regularization but redundant with the K masked views. PARK.

### REJECTED ON THE EVIDENCE

- **TENT / BatchNorm-statistic TTA.** The model uses LayerNorm, not BatchNorm, so TENT's BN-stat route needs an architecture change. Worse, TENT is entropy minimization, which sharpens confident predictions roughly monotonically → largely rank-preserving → invisible to a rank-only metric. Not worth it. (Wang et al., ICLR 2021.)
- **DANN-style domain-adversarial encoder (gradient reversal).** Making encoder features train/test-indistinguishable removes the very backscatter-LEVEL signal that is both discriminative and shifted; with adv-AUC 0.99 the discriminator dominates. High risk of deleting the pond signal along with the nuisance. Reject (same failure family as importance weighting).
- **CORAL / OT moment alignment applied to the level features.** Aligning global feature moments across train/test can destroy the absolute-level signal that finding (C) proved is the class signal. Only defensible if restricted to a normalized subspace, which is speculative; reject the naive form.
- **Temperature scaling / focal loss / label smoothing as LB levers.** Rank-invisible (proof above). Correct to reject.
- **Further λ sweeping, positional reframes, K=2→K=4, per-cell detrend, water indices (WIF/EVI/SDWI), Saerens–EM/BBSE/MLLS priors, GBDT/family blending.** All refuted previously and consistent with the seed-noise floor; no reason to revisit.

## Recommendations: the ranked top-5 and a 24-day plan

**The ordered strategies (expected private-rank gain × feasibility):**
1. **Variance-collapse rank-averaged ensemble** across seeds × folds × decorrelated architectures, combined by rank not probability, with the prevalence pin re-derived on seed-averaged OOF. This is the primary private-LB weapon and doubles as reproducibility-rubric insurance.
2. **In-domain transductive SSL pretraining**, gated by the free adversarial-AUC test — the only model-class-scale attack on the shift that is legal and unexplored, and the strongest Phase-Two novelty story.
3. **Dispersion-aware (moment) pooling** — physics-motivated, capacity-neutral, and the dispersion invariant should transfer across periods.
4. **MiniRocket/Hydra decorrelated member** — bought for ensemble diversity, not solo level; include only if rank-corr with the transformer is low.
5. **Transductive consistency on unlabeled test rows** (Mechanism A), plus at most one capped, class-balanced, fold-safe pseudo-labeling round (Mechanism B) if screens are green.

**Day-by-day (≈24 days, budget ~60 remaining submissions, 2 finals):**
- **Days 1–3 (infrastructure, ~1 submission).** Build the automated offline screen harness: ATC-F1, DIS, and adversarial-train-vs-test-AUC. Reproduce champion at 5 seeds; confirm reliable ≈0.8865. One sanity submission.
- **Days 4–7 (free/cheap screening, ≤1 submission/day).** Run the free adversarial-AUC gate on in-domain SSL and on ROCKET features; run OOF+ATC-F1 for dispersion pooling and transductive-Var. Submit only candidates that pass their offline gate.
- **Days 8–14 (fund the winners, ~2 submissions/day).** Full 5-fold × multi-seed training of the 2–3 gate-passers. Compute the cross-model rank-correlation matrix. Begin assembling the decorrelated rank-averaged ensemble.
- **Days 15–20 (assemble + operating point).** Rank-average the decorrelated pool; re-derive P̂ per model on seed-averaged OOF; screen the pseudo-labeling round; lock the low-variance ensemble.
- **Days 21–24 (final selection + reproducibility).** Choose the 2 finals: (i) the low-variance rank-averaged decorrelated ensemble (private-optimized primary); (ii) a hedge that is *decorrelated* from (i) and has top ATC-F1 — NOT your highest single public score. Reserve the last day for a seeded end-to-end reproducibility run (required for top-5 code review).

**Go/no-go benchmarks:**
- In-domain SSL: fund only if adversarial AUC on embeddings drops below ~0.95 (from the 0.965 Presto floor); else PARK and reallocate to ensemble diversity.
- Any new architecture: worth a submission only if ATC-F1 ≥ champion − 0.005 (ATC resolution ~0.010).
- Ensemble member inclusion: mean pairwise rank-corr < 0.9 (target < 0.75) AND solo ATC-F1 within ~0.02 of champion.
- Pseudo-labeling: DIS must not worsen; cap 1 iteration; abandon if worst-fold OOF drops.

**What would change the plan:**
- If the SSL gate fails, drop it entirely and pour the freed submissions into more decorrelated ensemble members (ROCKET, dispersion-pool, NoPE, λ3).
- If day-to-day public scores of the leaders are volatile (evidence they are riding u), lean harder into variance collapse and worry less about closing the last 0.01 of public level.
- If a decorrelated member shows ρ<0.6 with ATC in range, prioritize the ensemble over SSL.
- If you are safely inside top-5 by ~Day 18, stop chasing LB and invest remaining time in the Phase-Two writeup (35% of the final score).

**Which of your findings I am challenging, explicitly:**
- **(A) "Only model-class changes clear the noise floor; an idea needs ≥0.02."** Correct for *level* ideas, wrong as a universal filter. Variance-reduction and combiner changes (rank-averaging, seed×fold×architecture collapse) have ~0 *level* effect yet a large *private-rank* effect via order statistics — they are unfundable by your bar but are the single highest-EV move. The bar mis-scores anything whose payoff is on the private slice.
- **(B) "The OOF→LB gap is mostly irreducible covariate shift."** The shift is real, but "irreducible" conflates *distribution* distance with *rank* transfer. Under a rank-only metric you only need within-test ordering, so high adversarial AUC (a distinguishability measure) does not bound achievable LB. In-domain transductive SSL and dispersion pooling target rank-relevant, period-invariant structure and are not ruled out by the adversarial evidence.
- **(E) "Ensembling buys variance, not level."** True *only* for your 95.1%-correlated members: the variance-reduction factor (1+ρ(M−1))/M = 0.96 at ρ=0.951 mathematically forces landing at the member mean. DiWA proves the gain grows as covariance falls, and Model Soups/Ovadia show the OOD level gain of *decorrelated* averaging exceeds its ID gain. Your open question ("does pooling decorrelated architectures buy level?") has a literature-backed answer: yes, under shift, conditional on ρ being low — which is exactly the regime you are now entering.
- I **accept (C)** (amplitude/level is the signal; do not normalize it away) and **(D)** (metric is rank-only), with the one refinement that the *operating-point pin* is visible even though calibration is not.

## Caveats
- Every historical LB delta in the ledger was measured with a ±0.019 instrument; the claim "only 2 effects exceeded the floor" is itself noisy, and absence of a measured effect is not proof of absence — especially for variance/robustness moves whose payoff is on the *private* slice and structurally invisible on public.
- The order-statistics argument assumes competitors are largely single-seed and select on public noise; if the top teams are already seed-averaging disciplined, the variance-collapse edge shrinks (though it never becomes negative, and it still satisfies the reproducibility rule).
- The in-domain SSL upside rests on 2,847 rows being enough to shift the encoder's manifold; this is genuinely uncertain and the free gate is there precisely to kill it cheaply if it does not.
- I could not access the Zindi discussion forum threads or the private challenge construction notes (JS-gated / login-walled); the challenge *rules and evaluation* were read directly from the public page, but the exact regional/temporal split construction is not public, so the "which invariant the organizers intended to reward" question is inferred from the aquaculture physics literature rather than confirmed from challenge documentation.
- The DomainBed/soup OOD gains (ViT-G/14 greedy soup +0.34 OOD vs +0.16 ID) are on image benchmarks, not S1/S2 pixel series; they establish the *mechanism and direction* (decorrelated averaging gains more under shift) but the *magnitude* here must be measured, not assumed.
- Rice phenology direction was corrected during review: the pond-vs-rice discriminator is the *seasonal backscatter swing / temporal variance* of rice (low at flooding, rising through heading, changing around drainage/harvest), not a uniform "sharp rise at harvest"; the robust claim is that ponds stay permanently low while rice varies — which is what dispersion-aware pooling exploits.
