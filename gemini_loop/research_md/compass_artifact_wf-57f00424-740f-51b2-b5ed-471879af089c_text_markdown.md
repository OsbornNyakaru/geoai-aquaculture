# GeoAI Aquaculture Pond ID — Round #17 Deep-Research Brief (10 days, ~66 submissions)

## TL;DR
- **The single highest-leverage under-explored lever is the tree/GBDT lane** — not because CatBoost is magic, but because the leader's "relative/ratio features" plus your OOF-0.995→LB-0.70 collapse are a textbook symptom of a model keying on n-dependent/shift-carrying columns. A correctly *shift-regularized* CatBoost on an n-invariant feature set is the most likely path past 0.906 and toward the leader's ~0.94. Re-open it as top priority.
- **Feature engineering is genuinely OPEN, not exhausted.** The winning `1[VH<−21]` channel is literally MiniRocket's PPV (proportion-of-positive-values) pooling; the whole ROCKET/MiniRocket + catch22-safe-subset + CDF-of-ratio family is the same *safe, n-invariant, threshold-crossing* coordinate class and is almost untested here. Ratios are affine-dead ONLY because of mean-pool — thresholds/CDFs of ratios are NOT affine and are usable today.
- **One minimal architecture edit (Linear→GELU→mean-pool) breaks the affine commutation** and immediately makes variance/covariance/ratio features usable; pair it with mean+std+IQR pooling and SAM. For finalists, designate `champion_perm_seedavg5_st` (robust) + `champion_archblend4` (decorrelated) — the E[max]-of-two-draws math favors the decorrelated low-variance pair over the lucky single seed, whose 0.9065 is the top of its own 0.879–0.907 spread.

## Key Findings

1. **Your negative results are statistically under-powered, and the correct protocol re-opens them.** With seed variance 0.019 and LB resolution ~0.013, a single-seed −0.006/−0.015/−0.018 is one draw from a distribution wider than the effect itself. Every parked "additions-only, single-seed" verdict lives inside the noise floor. The correct protocol is seed-averaged (≥5 seeds), tested as a *replacement* at constant channel width — never a single-seed addition.

2. **`1[VH<−21]` is a principled instance of a whole winning feature class, not a one-off.** MiniRocket (Dempster, Schmidt & Webb, KDD 2021, arXiv:2012.08791) achieves near-SOTA on the UCR archive using *only* PPV = mean_t 1[conv(x) > bias]. ROCKET (Dempster, Petitjean & Webb, arXiv:1910.13051) states verbatim: *"We found that ppv produces meaningfully higher classification accuracy than other features, including the mean (broadly equivalent to global average pooling)"* and calls ppv *"the single element of the Rocket architecture that is most critical to its outstanding accuracy."* Your permanence channel is a PPV feature on the raw VH series with a physically-anchored bias. This is strong evidence there are MORE such coordinates to mine, not fewer.

3. **The affine blind spot is real but is a property of the architecture, not the data.** Because a per-timestep Linear map and masked mean-pool commute, the model only sees affine functions of the temporal mean — so raw ratios/differences collapse. Inserting ANY elementwise nonlinearity before the pool (Linear→GELU→mean) breaks the commutation. This is the single cheapest way to make the entire ratio/variance/cross-band family usable.

4. **CatBoost's OOF-0.995→LB-0.70 is diagnosable and fixable.** OOF 0.995 with a 0.89 adversarial-AUC means the trees found train-only structure — near-certainly per-calendar-month columns, counts, min/max/range, and other n-dependent statistics that encode "how many months observed" (a shift carrier) rather than "what kind of pixel." Grinsztajn 2022 (arXiv:2207.08815) shows verbatim that *"tree-based models remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized data (~10K samples)"* and win by being *"robust to uninformative features"* and *"able to easily learn irregular functions"* — but only if the features are transferable. A correctly constrained CatBoost is therefore expected to transfer.

## Details

### §3 — The open feature-engineering lane (ranked by expected value)

**§3a SAR aquaculture/water/rice features (highest EV; TargetF1 + TargetRAUC).**
- *Permanence / PPV persistence (CONFIRMED winner class).* mean_t 1[VH_dB < τ]. Xing et al. 2018 (Dongting Lake, PeerJ 6:e4992, DOI 10.7717/peerj.4992) empirically derived, verbatim, *"The thresholds were −21.56 and −15.82 dB for the backscattering coefficients for VH and VV, respectively, and the overall accuracy and Kappa coefficients were above 95.50% and 0.90…for the VH…and above 94.50% and 0.88…for the VV."* Your −21 line is this VH threshold. **Note the caveat:** Xing's site is a freshwater lake, not managed aquaculture ponds, so treat −21.56/−15.82 dB as calibrated *water/non-water* cuts, not pond-specific. **Recipe:** add a VV-permanence twin `mean_t 1[VV_dB < −15.82]` as a *replacement*-style channel, seed-averaged ×5. Physical basis: specular reflection off a smooth managed-pond surface sends microwaves away from the sensor → persistently low backscatter; open/rough water and flooded rice do not stay low.
- *Temporal dispersion / stability (strong; likely net-positive as a nonlinear-in-mean feature).* Managed ponds hold a stable smooth surface; flooded rice swings ~6–8 dB across the phenological cycle (flooding minimum → tillering/heading maxima at VH). Sentinel-1 rice studies confirm VH shows the largest, most sustained seasonal swing (Fogera/Ethiopia, ScienceDirect S235293851930059X; ratoon-rice characterisation, T&F 10.1080/15481603.2025.2455081). Ottinger's operational Asia pipeline (Ottinger, Bachofer, Huth & Kuenzer, Remote Sens. 2022, 14(1):153, DOI 10.3390/rs14010153) computes, verbatim, *"the median; standard deviation; 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile"* of the S1 cube, noting *"The pixel-wise median (50th percentile) is particularly suitable for identifying permanent and stable high (dams, dikes) and permanent low scatterers (smooth water surface)."* **Safe encodings under the masking trap:** temporal IQR (Q75−Q25) or MAD of VH — n-robust, unlike raw SD/range. This is nonlinear-in-the-mean, so it survives the current mean-pool the same way permanence does.
- *Dike double-bounce (moderate, low EV here).* Managed ponds are bounded by dikes; the water-dike corner elevates VV and VH/VV. With isolated pixels and no neighborhood you cannot see geometry, so this is only capturable distributionally (high VV-quantile co-present with low VH-quantile). Lower EV.

**§3b Short-time-series features safe at n=4–6 (high EV, both columns).** The catch22 set (Lubba et al. 2019, arXiv:1901.10200, DMKD 33:1821) is z-scored and by design ignores location/spread; most of its 22 features are autocorrelation/fluctuation statistics that are UNSTABLE at n=5 and encode ordering that your correlated dropout destroys. **Do NOT port catch22 wholesale.** The safe subset is the *distributional / order-statistic* family: PPV / threshold-crossing fractions (MiniRocket), interior quantiles, CDF values, L-moments (L-scale and L-skew are far more robust at small n than ordinary moments), and U-statistics. These are simultaneously n-invariant and mean-pool-surviving. The "Stats" (per-patch mean/SD) augmentation to catch22 helps long UCR series but is n-fragile here — skip.

**§3c Optical features that survive cloud gaps (moderate–high EV, mostly TargetF1).** Raw NDVI/NDWI/MNDWI/NDRE as per-month inputs are ratios → affine-dead in the current model. As **occupancy/CDF features they become new coordinates**: `ever_green_frac = mean_t 1[NDVI > 0.3]` (rejects open water, flags vegetation/rice months) and `open_water_frac = mean_t 1[MNDWI > 0]`, where MNDWI = (Green−SWIR1)/(Green+SWIR1) and the zero cut is the standard, built-up/soil-robust water threshold (more reliable than NDWI). Managed ponds → high open_water_frac AND low ever_green_frac across observed months; flooded rice → high open_water_frac in flooding months BUT high ever_green_frac later. This NDVI-occupancy contrast is the single most surgical optical rice-rejector, is naturally n-invariant (a mean over whatever months are present), and is nonlinear so it survives both cloud gaps and mean-pool.

**§3d Cross-sensor SAR×optical fused features (high EV, both columns).** SAR×optical fusion consistently adds ~3–7 points of OA over either sensor alone for paddy/aquaculture separation (soybean S1×S2 fusion OA 96.85% with a +6.96% F1 gain at the flowering stage when optical is constrained, PMC12580376; typhoon-lodged-rice SAR+optical 91.29% vs 88.4%/85.2% single-source, PMC7767387). The surgical fused statistic for THIS problem is a **joint-occupancy gate**: `pond_frac = mean_t ( 1[VH_dB<−21] · 1[MNDWI>0] )` — the fraction of observed months that are simultaneously SAR-smooth-water AND optically-water, strictly more selective than either marginal and a genuinely new nonlinear coordinate. Rice-rejecting complement: `mean_t ( 1[VH_dB<−21] · 1[NDVI<0.2] )`.

**§3e "Relative/ratio" features (the leader's stated lever), translated to survive mean-pool (high EV — this is the leader's edge).** Raw VH−VV, VH/VV, RVI = 4·VH/(VV+VH), SDWI = ln(10·VV·VH) collapse under mean-pool. Their **thresholded/CDF forms do not**: `mean_t 1[(VH−VV)<c]`, `mean_t 1[RVI<r]`, plus **within-series relative position** (rank of each month's ratio) and **ratio dispersion** (IQR of VH−VV over time). Physical basis: RVI and VH−VV separate smooth water (low depolarization) from vegetated/rough surfaces; a pond stays low-RVI persistently, rice sweeps low→high. The leader's CatBoost can use raw ratios *because trees threshold internally* — which is exactly why porting thresholded ratios into your model, OR fixing the pooling (§5), closes that gap.

### §4 — Parked paths: re-open verdicts and proper re-tests

1. **Second feature channels — RE-OPEN (high priority).** Their −0.006/−0.015/−0.018 were single-seed additions inside a 0.019 floor. **Proper re-test:** seed-average ×5, and test the new channel as a *replacement* holding total width at the 25-channel sweet spot (drop one weak learned channel, insert VV-permanence or the joint-occupancy gate). Also try *softening* (learned temperature on the binary via a sigmoid). Expected effect ~+0.003–0.008: a coin-flip against the floor per channel, but the seed-average removes the variance that killed the earlier read. **Submission — but only after the offline seed-mean is computed.**

2. **VH−VV / ratios / SDWI / RVI — RE-OPEN as thresholds/CDFs, not raw.** Parked as "affine-spanned"; TRUE for raw ratios under mean-pool, FALSE for `mean_t 1[ratio<c]`. **Proper re-test:** add `mean_t 1[(VH−VV)<c]` for c at the train median of VH−VV, seed-averaged, as a replacement. If it moves nothing, that is real evidence the ratio axis is saturated; if it moves, it corroborates the leader. **Submission.**

3. **Trees / GBDT (CatBoost) — RE-OPEN, TOP PRIORITY.** The OOF-0.995→LB-0.70 collapse is the signature of keying on shift-carrying features. **Correct reconstruction:**
   - *Feature set:* n-invariant ONLY — means, medians, interior quantiles (Q25/Q50/Q75), the PPV/occupancy fractions from §3, IQR/MAD, L-moments. **BAN** min, max, range, counts, run-lengths, argmax-timing, per-calendar-month columns, and any raw n.
   - *Shift-aware CV:* do NOT use random k-fold (it leaks the shift). Train an is-test classifier (adversarial validation) and weight/stratify folds by test-likeness so CV rewards transfer, not memorization.
   - *Regularization (heavy):* depth 3–4 (not 6+), l2_leaf_reg 10–30 (default 3), random_strength 2–5, subsample/rsm 0.6–0.8, learning_rate 0.02–0.03, iterations with early stopping on the shift-weighted fold, plus **monotonic constraints** on physically-signed features (pond-probability non-decreasing in open_water_frac and permanence; non-increasing in ever_green_frac / RVI-dispersion). Monotone constraints are a strong shift regularizer — they forbid the non-physical wiggles that memorize train noise.
   - *Prevalence:* fit toward the ~0.65 test prior via Saerens correction (§6), not the 0.40 train prior.
   Expected effect: the widest-tailed lever — plausibly 0.02–0.04 IF the collapse was feature-driven (likely), i.e., a credible path to the leader region. **Submission, gated on the shift-weighted CV first (free offline check).**

4. **Multi-τ / dense CDF profile — RE-OPEN with a bottleneck.** The monotone 1τ>4τ>6τ result was single-seed AND additions-only, so extra τ's added correlated capacity that overfits the shift. **Proper re-test:** feed a dense CDF profile (8–16 τ's) THROUGH a low-rank/1-unit bottleneck or with channel dropout, so the model gets the *shape* without the capacity blow-up. If a regularized bottleneck beats 1τ, the axis was capacity-limited, not saturated. **Offline check first, then one submission.**

5. **Learnable / adaptive / per-window τ — RE-OPEN cautiously (lower priority).** "Drifts to a train statistic; Otsu ill-posed at n=5" is right for a *free* threshold. Hard-concrete gates (Louizos, Welling & Kingma, ICLR 2018, arXiv:1712.01312) give a differentiable, anchored way to learn a binary gate; anchor/constrain it to a small ρ-ball around the physical −21 dB so it cannot drift to a train-only value (straight-through estimation is the alternative gradient path). Published evidence is in-distribution only (no OOD gain demonstrated), so rank below 1–4. **Offline check only.**

6. **Foundation-model embeddings (Presto etc.) — KEEP PARKED.** Adv-AUC 0.97 and magnitude dependence mean they encode the shift; no scale/shift-invariant fine-tuning recipe in the verified literature transfers to isolated-pixel tiny-n. Not worth a submission.

7. **Test-time adaptation / pseudo-labeling — RE-OPEN one narrow variant (offline gate).** Full TTA collapses at adv-AUC 0.9. The safe variants with published high-shift gains are class-balanced self-training (CBST, Zou et al. ECCV 2018) and confidence-regularized self-training (CRST, Zou et al. ICCV 2019) — the winning core of the CVPR 2018 WAD domain-adaptation challenge. Key: class-balanced pseudo-label selection (normalize confidence per class so the 0.65-prevalence majority does not dominate) + abstention on low-confidence pixels. Risk remains high at your shift level. **Offline check only; do not spend a submission unless the offline gate is strongly positive.**

8. **Prior-shift / Saerens correction — RE-OPEN; now decidable and legal (see §6).**

### §5 — If the model is the bottleneck

**Q1 — Pooling / architecture (ranked).**
- **Linear→GELU→masked-mean-pool (minimal edit, highest leverage).** One elementwise nonlinearity before the pool breaks the Linear/mean commutation, making variance, cross-band, and ratio information representable with almost no capacity added — directly unlocking §3d/§3e. Cost: a small hit to the pure-affine model's clean shift-robustness; mitigate with the Q2 regularizers. **One submission after offline seed-mean.**
- **Mean + std + IQR pooling.** Concatenate masked mean, masked SD, and masked IQR of the per-timestep encodings. Ottinger's operational pipeline uses exactly median+SD+percentiles; std/IQR pooling makes temporal dispersion (the pond-vs-rice discriminator) first-class, and IQR keeps it n-robust. Low capacity, high physical motivation.
- **Distribution/quantile pooling.** Replace mean-pool with a fixed grid of interior quantiles of the per-timestep encodings (quantile layers; distributional-embedding theory, arXiv:1912.01933). The quantile function uniquely determines the distribution, so this is strictly more expressive than mean while staying permutation-invariant and n-robust.
- **Set Transformer PMA (Lee et al., ICML 2019, arXiv:1810.00825).** PMA pools a set with k learned seed queries via multihead attention; with k=1 it yields a single permutation-invariant embedding and provably generalizes sum/mean pooling (its Lemma shows PMA can express sum-pooling). More capacity → more shift risk at n≈1800; use only if mean+std and GELU-pool plateau.
- **Deep Sets with a nonlinear per-element encoder (Zaheer et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.06114).** ρ(mean φ(x)) with nonlinear φ is the general form; your current model is the degenerate linear-φ case, so adopting a 2-layer φ is equivalent in spirit to the GELU edit.

**Q2 — Capacity control that ENABLES a shifting feature (mechanism-level).**
- **SAM / flat minima (Foret, Kleiner, Mobahi & Neyshabur, ICLR 2021, arXiv:2010.01412; ρ≈0.05).** Perturb weights to the worst case in a ρ-ball (ε̂ = ρ·∇L/‖∇L‖), then step at the perturbed point — seeks flat minima at ~2× SGD cost. The original paper states, verbatim, that *"SAM natively provides robustness to label noise on par with…state-of-the-art procedures that specifically target learning with noisy labels"* (CIFAR at 20/40/60/80% noise). Independent OOD studies (arXiv:2412.05169; WILDS/PACS in arXiv:2211.08583) report further out-of-distribution gains — *directional but I could not independently verify the specific magnitudes, so treat the exact percentages as unconfirmed.* Mechanism for your case: flat minima tolerate the train↔test coordinate wobble of an informative-but-shifting feature, turning a −0.006 addition into a net win. **Recommended regularizer to pair with any new channel or the GELU edit.**
- **SWAD (Cha et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2102.08604).** Dense, overfit-aware weight averaging; SOTA on 5 DG benchmarks (+1.6% OOD avg) explicitly by finding flat minima, and works *without* domain labels. Buys LEVEL, not just variance reduction (see Q4).
- **Dropout = adaptive L2 (Wager, Wang & Liang, NIPS 2013, arXiv:1307.1493).** Dropout is first-order equivalent to L2 after scaling features by inverse-Fisher — an *adaptive* penalty proportional to feature scale. Channel dropout on input features is therefore the principled way to let the model down-weight a high-variance shifting channel without discarding it. In-distribution evidence only, but the mechanism is precisely "absorb a noisy feature."
- **Mixup / Manifold Mixup (arXiv:1710.09412 / 1806.05236; RegMixup arXiv:2206.14502, NeurIPS 2022).** Interpolating inputs+labels reduces memorization of corrupt signal and improves calibration under covariate shift (CIFAR-10/100-C); Manifold Mixup interpolates hidden states → flatter representations and low-confidence regions between classes. Helps TargetF1 (robustness) and TargetRAUC (calibration). Cheap to add.
- **Low-rank input bottleneck** on a dense CDF/quantile profile: absorbs shape without capacity — the fix for parked path #4.

### §6 — The other live levers

**Q3 — Legal operating point (Saerens prior correction).** Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker (Neural Computation 2002, 14(1):21–41) give the EM update: initialize test prior q⁽⁰⁾ = train prior; E-step reweights each posterior p(y|x) by the ratio q/p and renormalizes; M-step sets q to the mean adjusted posterior; iterate to convergence. **Legality:** this is a *prior* correction applied to probabilities *before* the literal 0.5 cut, estimated from train OOF + unlabeled test only — it is NOT threshold tuning (the 0.5 cut is untouched) and uses no test labels or LB feedback, so it is within the rules. Because your F1 is at a fixed 0.5 cut and true prevalence is ~0.65 vs train 0.40, correcting the prior shifts probabilities across 0.5 in the right direction — the only legal way to gain F1 under label shift. **Safest π_t estimator ranking:** (1) a **physics-anchored** estimate from the VH-permanence marginal (the fraction of test pixels with pond-consistent permanence is a nearly model-free prevalence proxy and the most shift-robust); (2) **MLLS with bias-corrected calibration** (Alexandari, Kundaje & Shrikumar, ICML 2020, arXiv:1901.06852 — its title is literally *"Maximum Likelihood with Bias-Corrected Calibration is Hard-To-Beat at Label Shift Adaptation"*); (3) **BBSE** (Lipton, Wang & Smola, ICML 2018, arXiv:1802.03916) — confusion-matrix inversion, but it degrades when a rare class grows and needs an invertible confusion matrix, so it is *less* safe here than MLLS. **Diagnostic (pure-label vs conditional shift):** check whether p(x|y) is stable by comparing class-conditional feature distributions on train OOF; given adv-AUC 0.89, conditional shift likely contributes, so damp the correction toward the physics-anchored π rather than trusting MLLS/BBSE fully, and gate the whole thing on an offline mixture goodness-of-fit test. Expected F1 upside is potentially >0.013 (the prevalence gap is large), so **worth a submission — but only the gated, damped version.**

**Q4 — Robust averaging.** You measured a REAL +0.0055 LEVEL gain from 5-seed permanence averaging — the ensemble mean beating the member mean, consistent with decorrelated errors. SWA (Izmailov et al., arXiv:1803.05407) / SWAD (arXiv:2102.08604) buy level in *weight space* (flat minima) rather than *output space*, at a fraction of the inference cost of seed ensembling, and SWAD's DG evidence says the level gain transfers OOD. **Recommendation:** run SWAD *within* each seed and ensemble across a few seeds — flatness + decorrelation stack. For a hard-0.5 F1 metric, the best operator is the one that stabilizes the probability near 0.5, which weight-averaging does better than a small output ensemble.

**Q5 — Finalists (E[max] order-statistics).** Final rank = the BETTER of your two picks on private-721, so selection pays for BOTH mean and decorrelation. Model each candidate's private score as Normal(μ, σ), with σ shrinking as ~0.019/√(ensemble size). For two candidates, E[max(X₁,X₂)] = μ̄ + a term that grows with both the spread between their means and their individual σ, and — critically — with their *de*correlation. Two decorrelated mid-μ models therefore beat one high-variance lucky seed paired with a near-duplicate.
- `c_perm_single` 0.906492: high point estimate, but a *single-seed* draw whose own seed distribution spans 0.879–0.907 (σ≈0.011); as a private-set estimator its μ regresses toward the 5-seed mean 0.8999, not 0.9065.
- `champion_perm_seedavg5_st` 0.899882: μ≈0.900, small σ (already 5-seed averaged) — the robust anchor.
- `champion_archblend4` 0.899643: μ≈0.900 and *decorrelated* from the permanence line (4 architectures) — the diversity leg.
**Designate `champion_perm_seedavg5_st` + `champion_archblend4`.** They share essentially the same μ (≈0.900, near the lucky seed's *true* μ), but the pair maximizes E[max] because their errors are decorrelated and their individual σ are small — you buy two nearly-independent shots at the top of the distribution instead of one lucky shot plus its own noise. Choosing `c_perm_single` trades a genuine decorrelation leg for a point estimate that is mostly seed luck (0.9065 = top of its own 0.879–0.907 spread; regression-to-mean says it underperforms on private). Only override if a new model shows BOTH a higher seed-mean AND decorrelation from the permanence line.

## Recommendations (staged, with go/no-go thresholds)

**Guiding rule:** OOF is anti-correlated with LB here, so pre-screen with **shift-weighted CV / adversarial validation and seed-means**, never plain OOF. Spend a submission only after an offline gate passes. Only effects >0.013 are LB-resolvable.

**Days 1–2 (offline, 0 submissions):**
- Build the n-invariant feature bank (§3: VV-permanence, temporal IQR/MAD of VH, NDVI-occupancy, MNDWI-occupancy, joint-occupancy gates, thresholded ratios, interior quantiles, L-moments).
- Stand up shift-weighted adversarial-validation CV.
- Reconstruct CatBoost per §4.3 (depth 3–4, l2_leaf_reg 10–30, monotone constraints, ~0.65 prior). **Go/no-go:** shift-weighted CV F1 must beat the train-prior baseline by a clear margin AND the adversarial gap must close vs the OOF-0.995 run. If not → the shift is conditional, deprioritize trees.

**Days 3–5 (submissions 1–8, ≤5/day):**
- **Sub 1: seed-averaged shift-regularized CatBoost — the top-EV shot.** Go/no-go: clears ~0.88 → it is transferring, invest more; collapses again → shift is conditional not label, pivot to the Transformer.
- Sub 2: Transformer + VV-permanence twin as a *replacement* (seed-avg 5).
- Sub 3: joint-occupancy gate channel (seed-avg 5).
- Sub 4: Linear→GELU→mean-pool + SAM (ρ=0.05), seed-avg 5.
- Subs 5–8: mean+std+IQR pooling; thresholded-ratio channel; best-two combined.

**Days 6–7 (submissions 9–13):**
- Saerens prior correction (physics-anchored π first, gated by mixture GoF) on the current best model — one submission; then the MLLS-BBC variant if positive.
- SWAD within-seed + cross-seed ensemble of the best Transformer.

**Days 8–9 (submissions 14–18):**
- Combine winners: shift-CatBoost ⊕ GELU-pool Transformer (decorrelated blend), seed+SWAD averaged, Saerens-corrected.
- Dense-CDF-through-bottleneck if earlier signal warranted it.

**Day 10:** freeze; designate finalists `champion_perm_seedavg5_st` + `champion_archblend4` unless a new model has both a higher seed-mean AND decorrelation from the permanence line (then it replaces the weaker leg).

**Most likely underrated because shelved too early:**
1. **Shift-regularized CatBoost.** The one-run collapse was almost certainly feature contamination (n-dependent / per-month columns), not a verdict on trees — and the leader proves the lane is alive. This is the single biggest miss.
2. **Thresholded/CDF-of-ratio features + the GELU-pool edit.** Ratios were declared dead on an architectural technicality that a one-line nonlinearity removes — and ratios are literally the leader's stated lever.

## Caveats
- **Anti-correlated OOF is the central hazard.** Every recommendation is gated on shift-aware offline checks, not OOF; treat any lever that only improves OOF as suspect.
- **The 0.013 floor is unforgiving.** Single-channel feature edits mostly live near it; their value is realized only via seed-averaging and combination. Do not judge any one on a single submission.
- **CatBoost upside is a hypothesis, not a fact** — its wide tail cuts both ways. If shift-weighted CV does not close the adversarial gap, the shift is conditional (p(x|y) moves), and no prior correction or tree retuning will save it; in that regime the honest answer is that the Transformer + permanence family near 0.90 may be close to the data's ceiling, and the leader's 0.94 rests on *features* (relative/ratio, thresholded inside a tree) you must reconstruct, not on the model class alone.
- **Conditional vs label shift is the crux for §6.** Saerens/BBSE/MLLS are valid only under (approximate) pure label shift; adv-AUC 0.89 suggests meaningful conditional shift, so damp toward the physics-anchored prior and never LB-tune π.
- **Xing's thresholds are lake-derived, not pond-derived** — re-estimate the exact VH/VV cut on your own train OOF rather than hard-coding −21.56/−15.82; use them as priors, not constants.
